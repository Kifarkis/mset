This is the Article 28 agreement for every customer of an MSET product: the organisation that uses MSET Sign, MSET Trust or MSET Transcribe to handle other people's personal data. It is one agreement for all of them, shaped like MSET's privacy notice: a common body that applies to every product, and one annex per product that says exactly what that product processes, how long it keeps it and who helps us run it.

Provider / Processor: Design & Animation i Vellinge AB, org. no. 556825-4295, Skåne, Sweden, trading as MSET ("we", "us", "MSET")

Customer / Controller: the organisation that has accepted the terms of service of an MSET product ("you", "the Customer")

Effective from: the effective date published on this page, or the date you first accepted an MSET product's terms of service, whichever is later.

1. What this agreement is, and how it is entered into

1.1 One agreement for every product

This Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") forms part of the terms of service of each MSET product (each the "Terms"). It is the written contract required by Article 28(3) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR).

The common body (clauses 1 to 16) applies to every product. Each product has its own annex. An annex applies while you use that product, and only then.

1.2 How it is entered into

You enter into this DPA by accepting the Terms of any MSET product. No separate signature is required, and you do not need to negotiate it before you can use the service. One acceptance covers every MSET product you use, now or later: when you start using another product, its annex applies from that day, without a new acceptance.

If your organisation needs a countersigned copy for its own records, ask at info@mset.se and we will provide one.

1.3 Order of precedence

  • Where this DPA and the Terms of a product conflict on the processing of personal data, this DPA prevails.
  • Where the common body and a product's annex conflict for that product, the annex prevails.
  • Where this DPA conflicts with the Standard Contractual Clauses referred to in clause 9, the Standard Contractual Clauses prevail.

1.4 Terms used

Personal data, processing, controller, processor, sub-processor, data subject, personal data breach and supervisory authority have the meaning the GDPR gives them. "Product" means MSET Sign, MSET Trust or MSET Transcribe. "Annex" means the annex for a product, with its own Annexes I to III.

2. Roles

2.1 Where you are the controller and we are your processor

For the personal data your organisation puts into a product, or collects through it about other people — the people you send documents to sign, the visitors to your trust centre, the people in your meetings — you are the controller and we are your processor. This DPA governs that processing. The annex for each product says exactly what is processed, and where MSET is a controller instead.

2.2 Where we are the controller

For your account data — your organisation's details, the people at your organisation who use the products, how they sign in, your subscription and billing state, and our own security logs — we are the controller. That is outside this DPA; section 4 of MSET's privacy notice describes it. Your people sign in through MSET ID, MSET's identity service.

2.3 Where we are a controller in our own right for product data

In one case a product keeps something as our record, not yours: MSET Sign's evidence record of each signature (Annex S, clause S4). The annex says what it is, why it exists and why erasure stops at its edge.

2.4 Services of the same company

MSET ID, MSET Sign and MSET's commerce service are operated by the same company as every other MSET product. Where one product uses another, that is not a sub-processor: the processing is ours, under this DPA, as the annexes say.

3. What you are responsible for

You warrant that:

  • you have a lawful basis for the processing you instruct us to carry out;
  • you inform the people whose data you put into a product as Articles 13 and 14 GDPR require. Most of them have no relationship with us. MSET's privacy notice has a section for each product's data subjects to help you, but giving that information is your obligation as controller, not ours;
  • you do not put special categories of personal data (Article 9) or criminal-offence data (Article 10) into a product unless you have satisfied yourself that the measures in the annex's Annex II are appropriate for it, and you have a valid Article 9 or 10 condition;
  • the instructions you give us do not breach data protection law.

Each annex adds what is particular to its product.

4. Subject matter and duration

The subject matter, nature and purpose of the processing for each product are in its annex.

Processing under this DPA lasts for as long as you use a product, plus the period after your subscription ends that the product's annex states. Clause 12 says what happens at the end.

5. Our instructions and our limits

5.1 Documented instructions

We process personal data only on your documented instructions, including in relation to transfers to a third country, unless EU or Swedish law requires otherwise — in which case we will tell you before processing, unless that law prohibits it on important grounds of public interest.

Your documented instructions are: the Terms, this DPA, the configuration you set in each product, and what your administrators and users do in it. Anything else must be agreed in writing.

5.2 If we think an instruction is unlawful

We will tell you if, in our opinion, an instruction infringes the GDPR or other EU or Member State data protection law. We may suspend the affected processing until it is resolved.

5.3 What we will not do

We will not:

  • use your content, or the personal data in it, for our own purposes, except as an annex states (MSET Sign's evidence record) and for aggregated, fully anonymised operational statistics that cannot identify you or any person;
  • train machine-learning models on your content, for ourselves or anyone else, or permit a sub-processor to do so;
  • send your content to an AI model except as an annex states, only where you have switched that function on, and only under terms that forbid the provider to train on it;
  • sell, rent or share personal data processed under this DPA;
  • read your content, except as the annex for that product allows and within its limits.

6. Confidentiality and personnel

Everyone we authorise to process personal data under this DPA is bound by an obligation of confidentiality, contractual or statutory, that survives the end of their engagement.

Access to systems holding your data is restricted to those who need it to provide or secure the service, and privileged access is logged. Each annex says what our operators can see in that product, and how you allow more.

7. Security

We implement the technical and organisational measures in each annex's Annex II, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing, as Article 32 requires.

Across the products: encryption in transit, and at rest for our databases and stored documents; separation between customers; access limited to what each person needs; logs of privileged access; and no analytics or advertising cookies — only strictly necessary session and preference cookies.

We may update the measures, provided the level of protection is not reduced. MSET runs its information security management system according to ISO/IEC 27001:2022. Certification by an accredited body is planned. Our Statement of Applicability is available on request.

8. Sub-processors

8.1 General written authorisation

You give us general written authorisation to engage sub-processors, on the conditions in this clause. The sub-processors of each product are listed in its annex's Annex III. The lists are not published: we give them to you in MSET's trust centre and on request to info@mset.se, and every change reaches you as clause 8.3 says.

8.2 Our obligations towards them

Each sub-processor is engaged under a written contract imposing data protection obligations no less protective than those in this DPA. We remain fully liable to you for a sub-processor's performance as if it were our own.

8.3 Notice of change, and your right to object

We give at least 30 days' written notice before adding or replacing a sub-processor, by email to your account administrators and by updating the annex.

If you have a reasonable, data-protection-related objection, tell us within 30 days of the notice. We will work with you in good faith — by describing the safeguards, by offering an alternative configuration where one exists, or by not proceeding. If we cannot resolve it and the change is one we must make to keep providing the product, you may end the affected subscription with immediate effect. Payments are not refunded.

Where a change must be made urgently to protect security or continuity, we may make it first and notify you immediately, with the same objection right running from the notice.

8.4 What is not a sub-processor

These are not our sub-processors, and each annex names its own:

  • services of the same company (clause 2.4);
  • services you connect yourself — your own Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, your own Teams or Slack channel, your own systems receiving webhooks, your own AI provider, your own sign-in provider. They act for you, under your agreement with them;
  • providers that act under their own terms as controllers in their own right, where an annex says so;
  • our payment provider, which processes your subscription's payment. That is MSET's account data (clause 2.2).

9. International transfers

Where your data is. The products store your data at rest in a data centre in Sweden, in the EU. We will not move that storage outside the EU/EEA without notice. Each annex says what is stored where; for MSET Transcribe, your meetings stay in your own Microsoft 365.

That is not a claim that no personal data ever leaves the EEA, and we do not make it. Some of our providers are US companies or have US parents, and limited transfers or remote access can occur — for platform operation, mail delivery and support, and where you choose a provider in the United States. A message we send reaches its recipient wherever their mailbox is. Where personal data is transferred to or accessed from outside the EEA:

  • we rely on an adequacy decision where one covers the transfer, including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the recipient is certified under it; otherwise
  • we rely on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914), Module Three (processor to sub-processor) or the applicable module, incorporated into this DPA by reference for those transfers; and
  • we apply the supplementary measures Chapter V GDPR requires, including encryption in transit, access controls and the measures in each annex's Annex II.

Each annex's Annex III says which safeguard applies to which provider. If you need the Standard Contractual Clauses executed as a separate document, ask us and we will do it. On request we provide the information you reasonably need for your own transfer impact assessment.

10. Assistance: data-subject requests, security and impact assessments

10.1 Requests from data subjects

Taking into account the nature of the processing, we assist you by appropriate technical and organisational measures, insofar as possible, to respond to requests under Chapter III GDPR. In each product you can see, export and, where the annex says so, correct or delete the data yourself, without asking us — for most requests you will not need us at all. Each annex says how.

10.2 If a data subject contacts us instead

We will not answer the substance of a request about data we process for you. We will tell the person that you are the controller, identify you to them, and pass the request to you without undue delay.

10.3 People named inside your content

We do not inspect the content of your documents or meetings and cannot search it. If a request concerns someone named inside your content, you will need to identify the affected items; we will then act on your instruction about them.

10.4 Where erasure stops

Two records in the products are kept for a stated period because their value as evidence depends on the person they record not being able to remove them: MSET Sign's evidence record (Annex S, clause S4), which we keep as controller, and an MSET Trust centre's access log (Annex T, clause T7), which you keep as controller. Each annex says what they hold, how long and on what basis.

10.5 Security, breaches and impact assessments

We assist you in ensuring compliance with Articles 32 to 36 — security of processing, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation — taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to us. Our documentation for a data protection impact assessment is part of what clause 13 provides.

11. Personal data breaches

We notify you of a personal data breach affecting personal data we process for you without undue delay after becoming aware of it, and in any case within 48 hours, so that you have time to meet your own Article 33 obligation.

The notification describes, as far as known at the time and updated as we learn more: the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and a contact point.

You notify your supervisory authority and, where required, the people affected, because you are the controller; we provide the information and cooperation you reasonably need. Where we are the controller, we notify the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) within 72 hours ourselves.

We will not identify you publicly in connection with a breach without consulting you first, unless the law requires it.

12. Return and deletion at the end

Return is yours, and self-service. Each product lets you export your data while your subscription runs and in the period after it ends that its annex states. That is the return mechanism; you do not need to ask us.

Deletion follows on schedule. At the end of that period we delete your data as the annex says, unless EU or Swedish law requires us to keep it, or the annex says otherwise for a record we keep as controller. On request we confirm deletion in writing.

Deletion is permanent. We do not restore deleted data from backup for you. Export before your data is deleted.

13. Audit and information rights

We make available the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28, and allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, by you or an auditor you mandate. For a supplier of our size, this is how it works:

  • First, documentation. Our security documentation, this DPA, the sub-processor lists with their safeguards, and our answers to a reasonable security questionnaire, within 30 days of your request and at no charge, once in any 12 months.
  • Then, if that is not enough, an audit by agreement, on site or remote: on at least 30 days' written notice, during business hours, once in any 12 months, under confidentiality, and scoped so as not to compromise other customers' data or our security. You bear your own costs and our reasonable costs of supporting it.
  • Without those limits where a supervisory authority requires it, or after a personal data breach affecting your data.

Our Statement of Applicability under ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (clause 7) is part of that documentation. Where a certification or third-party audit report exists in future, providing it will satisfy this clause in the first instance.

14. Liability

Liability under this DPA is subject to the limitations in the Terms of the product concerned, except where the law does not permit them. In particular, nothing in the Terms or this DPA limits a data subject's rights under Article 82 GDPR, or either party's liability to a supervisory authority.

15. Governing law and disputes

Swedish law governs this DPA. Disputes go to the Swedish courts, with Malmö District Court (Malmö tingsrätt) as the court of first instance. Where a separately negotiated agreement provides for another law, another court or arbitration, that agreement prevails for the parties to it.

If any provision is invalid, the rest stands.

16. Changes to this DPA

We may update this DPA where a change in law, in a product or in our sub-processors requires it, on 30 days' notice by email to your account administrators, provided the change does not reduce your protection. Where it would, we will seek your agreement. Adding an annex for a product you do not use changes nothing for you until you use it.

Annex S — MSET Sign

This annex applies while you use MSET Sign, the electronic signature service: you send a document for signature, the service collects the signatures and produces a sealed PDF with a signing certificate and a tamper-evident audit trail.

S1. Roles in MSET Sign

S1.1 You are the controller, we are your processor

For the documents you send, their metadata, the people you send them to, everything signers enter or attach, the signing events, and all personal data contained in or attached to them, you are the controller and we are your processor. So are your contacts, your templates, and the key terms and tags you keep on a document.

S1.2 We are an independent controller of the evidence record

For the evidence record of clause S4 we act as an independent controller in our own right. This is the one place where our role changes after your instruction ends, and clause S4 explains why.

S1.3 Identity providers act under their own terms

Where you require it, a signer verifies their identity with an eID through a contracted eID broker, or with an ID document check at an identity verification provider (clause S7). Each provider handles the signer's verification on its own pages, under its own terms and privacy notice, and is responsible for it. They are named in Annex S-III for transparency. We receive only what Annex S-I lists.

S1.4 What you are responsible for in MSET Sign

In addition to clause 3:

  • Signers typically have no prior relationship with you or with us. Section 5 of MSET's privacy notice is written for them, but informing them is yours.
  • The ID document check processes the signer's images, including their face, at the provider. You decide whether that check is appropriate for your signers, and you need an Article 9 condition for it; the provider asks the signer for their consent before the check starts.
  • Whether a signature is enough for a given document, in a given country, is your decision.

S2. Subject matter, nature and purpose

Subject matter and nature: receiving your documents; distributing them for signature and approval; authenticating signers by control of the email address the link was sent to, an optional one-time PIN to the same mailbox, a sign-in through MSET ID, or, where you require it, an eID or an ID document check; recording the signing events; applying an electronic seal and, where a trusted timestamp authority can be reached, an RFC 3161 timestamp; producing the sealed PDF with its signing certificate and audit trail; storing and returning the result; and deleting it on schedule.

The signature levels, stated plainly. By default the signatures are simple electronic signatures under Article 3(10) of eIDAS. An eID or ID document check strengthens the evidence of who signed. It does not, on its own, make a signature an advanced or a qualified electronic signature, and we do not describe it as one. Qualified signatures (QES) are coming; this annex will be updated, with notice under clause 8.3, before they are offered.

Purpose: electronic signature, sealing, and the production and preservation of evidence that a document was signed.

Duration. For as long as you use MSET Sign, plus the retention tail. The tail is two different clocks, and they are not the same:

S3. Data subjects and personal data

S3.1 Data subjects

  • Your users — the people in your organisation who send documents, as they appear in your documents and their audit trails.
  • Signers, approvers and copy recipients — the people you ask to sign, approve or receive a copy. Most of them have no relationship with us and did not choose the service.
  • Your contacts — the people you send to, kept for suggestions as you type.
  • People named inside a document — anyone whose personal data appears in a file you upload. We never inspect document content, so we do not know who they are and cannot search for them.

S3.2 Personal data

  • Identifiers: names and email addresses, and any company or title you or a signer supply.
  • Signing events: when the link was sent and opened, when a person signed, approved or declined, any reason given, and the IP address at those moments.
  • Authentication: that a one-time PIN was issued and correctly entered — the PIN itself is not kept after use — or that the signer signed in through MSET ID.
  • The signature as drawn or typed, and everything a signer fills in or attaches.
  • Document content and attachments, of whatever kind you upload — unknown to us in nature and never inspected.
  • eID evidence, where you require an eID: the eID broker's signed answer, kept verbatim — the signer's name, birth date and, where the eID carries one, a national identity number — with the key that verified it and the IP address. The name the eID verified is sealed into the certificate.
  • ID document check, where you require it: the outcome, the verified first and last name, the type and issuing country of the document, and a fingerprint of the document number that cannot be turned back into the number. We do not receive the images, and the date of birth, national ID number and address the provider reads are not stored.
  • 21 CFR Part 11, where you use it: the meaning of each signature, and the register of people enrolled to sign.
  • Key terms and tags you keep on a document, including key terms read by AI (clause S6).
  • AI assistance, where you switch it on: what is sent is in clause S6; the content sent is not stored by us.

S4. The evidence record, and why erasure stops at its edge

S4.1 What the evidence record is

When a document's files are deleted — by your retention setting, by your instruction, or because your account closed — the content is destroyed and we keep a minimal record that the document existed and was signed:

  • the document's reference;
  • who signed it, when, and how each signer was identified — with the eID evidence where an eID was used;
  • the signing events of its audit trail;
  • a cryptographic fingerprint (hash) of the signed file.

It contains none of your document files.

S4.2 How long

Three years from the deletion of the document's files. For a 21 CFR Part 11 signature, the record is also kept until five years from completion, and goes only when both periods have passed; the certificate prints that date. Then the whole record is deleted together, the eID evidence with it.

S4.3 Why it exists

A counterparty holding a sealed PDF years later must be able to check it against something and be told whether it is genuine. If the record could be erased on request, anyone who later regretted a signature could destroy the only proof it happened — and a signing service whose evidence can be quietly unmade is not evidence of anything.

S4.4 The legal position we take

For the evidence record we act as an independent controller, not as your processor:

  • Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) — our legitimate interest, and that of every party relying on a sealed document, in the integrity and verifiability of the evidence we have produced, and in the establishment, exercise and defence of legal claims.
  • Exemption from erasure: Article 17(3)(e) — the processing is necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.
  • Objection: an Article 21 objection will be considered, but we expect the compelling legitimate grounds above ordinarily to override it, for the reason given.

S4.5 What this means in practice

We cannot honour an instruction — from you or from a data subject — to erase the evidence record, and your instruction to delete a document does not extend to it. We can and will erase everything else. Where a data subject asks us to erase it, we tell them plainly that we decline, on what basis, what the record contains, and that they may complain to IMY.

Because you cannot instruct us to remove it, treat it as a feature of the service you are buying, and tell your own data subjects about it. Section 5 of MSET's privacy notice explains it in plain language. If this is not acceptable for a particular use, do not use MSET Sign for it.

S5. Support access — yours to give, and it ends by itself

Our support staff cannot open your documents unless you grant access. A grant is:

  • yours — made by you; there is no way for us to make one ourselves;
  • scoped — one document by default, or, as a deliberate choice, your whole organisation;
  • read-only;
  • time-limited — 72 hours unless you set another period, and never more than 30 days;
  • revocable at any time;
  • logged — every look is written to your Activity, naming the operator by reference, and to our own operator log;
  • notified — we email you the first time a document is opened under it.

S6. AI assistance — off until you switch it on

  • Off by default. Your administrator chooses Off, MSET AI or your own AI provider. Nothing is sent to an AI while it is off.
  • MSET AI is operated by us, through a contracted AI provider your administrator chooses (Annex S-III); before they choose, the app tells them where each provider processes the text. Your own provider is under your own agreement with it, and is not our sub-processor.
  • What is sent, and only what the function needs: for placing fields, reading key terms and checking a document against your playbook, the text of the document's lines — never the file itself, and never signers' addresses; for the message to signers, the title, the file names, the number of signers, the language and your notes.
  • When: when a person presses an AI button — and, only if your administrator switches on automatic key terms, once for each signed document.
  • Recorded, not stored: each request writes a line in your Activity naming who asked, what kind of content went and to which provider. We do not store the content sent. Key terms read by AI are kept as your notes (Annex S-I), marked as read by AI.
  • No training. We do not use your documents to train any model, and the providers we use are bound not to.
  • AI output can be wrong. Nothing is sent to signers until a person presses Send.

S7. Identity checks

  • eID: Swedish BankID, MitID (including MitID Erhverv) and Norwegian BankID, through the eID broker named in Annex S-III, where your plan includes it and you require it. The verified name is what the signature carries; if it differs from the name you gave, the signer confirms it and you are told. The broker's answer is verified against its published keys before it is kept.
  • ID document check: for signers without one of those eIDs, the signer photographs an identity document and themselves at the provider named in Annex S-III. A check in progress is held for at most two days; what is kept of a completed one is in clause S3.2.
  • Both are part of the evidence record (clause S4) and are deleted with it.

S8. Services you connect

Microsoft Outlook, Teams and SharePoint; Google Gmail and Drive; Zapier and Make; your own systems through the API and webhooks; an AI agent through MCP; your own sign-in provider; and your own AI provider. A connection is yours to make and to remove, and what you send through it is governed by your agreement with that service. They are not our sub-processors. The add-ons act as the signed-in person, on the message or file they open; an API key does only what its scope allows; a webhook can be set to send only the reference, with no names, addresses or title. Webhooks and API keys are off until we switch them on for your organisation.

S9. Where your data is

Your documents, their metadata and the service database are stored at rest in a data centre in Sweden. Invitations reach signers wherever their mailbox is. Transfers are as clause 9 and Annex S-III say.

S10. Data-subject requests in MSET Sign

You can view, export and delete documents and their data yourself. Settings → Data → Export your data gives one ZIP: the people, documents, signers and their data, every original upload and every sealed PDF, the full hash chain of every document, templates and the activity feed. One signed document can be downloaded with its Evidence pack. The evidence record is outside erasure as clause S4 says.

S11. When your subscription ends, and when your account closes

When your subscription ends, sending stops and your documents become read-only: you can still read, download and export them. They are deleted at whichever comes first — your own retention period, or 90 days after the subscription ended. We write to you before that date and name it.

When your account closes, all document files and signed PDFs are permanently deleted, and so are your key terms and tags. The evidence record stays for the period in clause S4, on our own controller basis, not as your data. Export before you close.

Annex S-II — Technical and organisational measures (as built)

Encryption

  • TLS in transit across all interfaces; encryption at rest for the database and stored documents.
